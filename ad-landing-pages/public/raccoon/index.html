Why an Inspection Matters

Sounds in the attic can come from several animals. An inspection helps identify the animal and the right next steps.

Sounds aren't conclusive

Raccoons, squirrels, rats, and opossums can all make noise in an attic. An inspection helps identify the actual animal.

Check for dependent young

Before sealing any entry points, it's important to confirm no dependent young are left inside the structure.

Entry points vary

Roof vents, soffits, and gaps near the roofline are common access points. An inspection locates the specific openings.

Raccoon Removal FAQs

Common questions about suspected raccoons and how removal is assessed.

How do I know if it's a raccoon in my attic?

Sounds alone don't confirm raccoons — squirrels, rats, and opossums can make similar noise. An on-site inspection helps identify the animal responsible based on entry points, tracks, droppings, and the type of damage present.

How is raccoon removal assessed?

We assess the attic for activity, locate possible entry points, and review visible damage. Removal and exclusion are then recommended as options for the property based on what we find during the inspection.

What does raccoon removal cost?

Cost depends on the inspection findings and the scope of the work involved. After the assessment, we can walk you through the options and any associated pricing before anything is done.

How do you prevent raccoons from coming back?

Exclusion focuses on sealing entry points — but only after confirming the animals and any dependent young are out. Sealing too early can trap young inside, so checking for babies is an important step before any repairs are made.
